Tested live on hulakico.com (customer and Admin signed in) plus code review.

## 3. Medium

- **SEO**: the 404 page declares the homepage as its canonical; sign-in, sign-up, account and book pages have a generic title and no `<h1>`.
- **Security headers**: add `Strict-Transport-Security`; move the Content-Security-Policy from `'unsafe-inline'` scripts to per-request nonces; add an Origin check on JSON API routes as defence in depth (SameSite cookies already block most cross-site posts).
- **Drafts**: 8 abandoned drafts on one account. Auto-expire untouched drafts after 30 days and show "Continue where you left off" for the newest one only.
- **Contact duplicated**: on some shipments the company field repeats the contact name; skip it when identical.
- **Old text in history**: tracking rows saved before the 25 Oct change keep "Shipment is in transit now." wording (new rows are fine).

## 4. What works well

All 46 public pages return 200 with titles, descriptions, Open Graph, canonical, structured data and image alt text; robots and sitemap are correct; Admin pages are all reachable and role-gated; booking validation, phone and address intelligence, rate-card matching, readable AWBs, customer emails, tracking stops, CI with security and image scanning, auto-deploy with rollback, and Admin session hardening are in place.

## 5. "AI-powered" — where it really stands

Today the intelligence is rules, not machine learning:

- Carrier ranking = fixed weights (55 % price, 35 % speed, 10 % "reliability"); reliability is guessed from the carrier's name (DHL/FedEx 0.88, others 0.72).
- ETA risk shows the same 0.22 for every shipment.
- Document QC checks required fields only.

Machine learning needs real history (thousands of delivered shipments with timestamps). The right order:

1. **Data foundation (now)** — record every scan with time and place (done from 25 Oct), promised vs actual delivery date, carrier, lane, weight, holds and their reasons. Without this no model can learn.
2. **AI that works without history (next)** — language models (Claude API) for: HS-code and description suggestions on the customs invoice; restricted / prohibited goods screening per destination (would have flagged "Ayurvedic Medicine → US"); a customer help assistant that answers from the shipment's own tracking.
3. **Measured scores (after ~3 months of data)** — carrier on-time % per lane replaces the name guess; ranking weights tuned from what customers actually choose.
4. **Machine learning (after ~6–12 months / a few thousand deliveries)** — ETA prediction (gradient-boosted model on lane, carrier, season, weight, customs), delay-risk alerts, price recommendations, fraud/anomaly detection on bookings and payments, demand forecasting per lane.
5. **Own fleet** (schema already has `transportMode: OWN_FLEET`) — pickup scheduling and route optimisation for Kathmandu valley vans.

## 6. To grow toward a DHL/FedEx-scale operator

- PostgreSQL (the plan's stated target) and a background job queue (emails, carrier sync, reminders).
- Live carrier APIs and webhooks (Phase 6) so partner scans flow in automatically.
- Shipping labels: printable AWB label (PDF) with barcode and QR, pickup booking.
- Proof of delivery: photo and receiver name at delivery.
- SMS / WhatsApp notifications in Nepali and English.
- Business accounts: bulk CSV upload, monthly invoicing on credit, API keys for shops.
- Imports (abroad → Nepal) pricing; handover from other Nepal cities.
- Customer support desk linked to shipments (messages inbox exists).

## Recommended order

1. C1 fake payments → C2 customs invoice gate → C3 data clean-up.
2. H1 speed (database region) → H2 password reset + email verification → H3 2FA + audit log → H4 monitoring.
3. LLM features: restricted-goods screening and HS-code suggestions.
4. Medium items, then the growth list.
